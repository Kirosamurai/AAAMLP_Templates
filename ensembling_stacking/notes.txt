1. Ensembling is nothing but a cobination of differrent models. The simplest way
to combine models would be to just do an average.

Ensemble Prediction = (m1_proba + m2proba ........ + mnproba)/n
This is the simplest way of combining models.

imp: combination of models which are not highly correlated work better than the
combination of models which are highly correlated.

ex - model A - decision tree
     model B - random forest
     model C - logistic regression

     model A+C gives better results than A+B

2. If we do not have probabilities, we can combine predictions too by taking a vote.

3. why do these work? errors can be minimized when we take averages. 

4. probabilites can also be combined by weights as 
w1m1 + w2m2 + w3m3 ....... wnmn where (w1 + w2 ...... + wn = 1)

5. how do we optimize the combined models?
    Step 1: create folds
    Step 2: train models on folds x and predict on fold y
    Step 3: train models on folds y and predict on fold x (from scratch)
    Step 4: now,we take fold x and the predictions of fold x and create an optimization 
            function that tries to find the best weights so as to maximise AUC against 
            fold y.
    
6. Bagging - create small subsets of data and train multiple simple models.
7. Boosting - Boosting models work similar to bagging models except for the fact that
              consecutive models are trained on error residulas and tend to minimize the 
              errors of preceding models. This can lead to overfitting however, as it can learn the data.

8. For multi-class classification, voting is a decent approach, but when we have pred_probas, 
   combining them would work better.

9. Stacking - Stacking uses a meta-model to create predictions
    i.e. - multiple models are trained use K-fold cross validation.
           The results of these models are then used by the meta-model 
           to train weights in order to best combine them.
